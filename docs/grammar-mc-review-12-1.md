## Findings

Seven of nine questions primarily recognize an activity noun as a sentence
subject; two recognize an explicitly requested accusative activity complement.
None meaningfully tests choosing between two plausible nonfinite constructions
for different predicates. This is recognition practice, not independent form
production or comprehensive -mAk versus -mA usage practice.

No definite wrong key or two defensible supplied answers was identified in this
small review. That is not independent linguistic approval. The existing risk
queue contains none of these nine IDs; absence from that queue is not approval.

Recommended disposition: retain four as introductory form recognition, flag five
for replacement proposals. “Keep” describes teaching purpose, not release-ready
linguistic status. “Revise” does not authorize deleting or modifying the originals.

## Source and terminology check

Consulted the supplied Yeni Hitit 1, printed p.145 (Adlaştırma table) and the
Unit XII grammar-support discussion of nominalization and case. These distinguish
nominal forms from finite forms and include infinitive subjects, infinitive +
istemek, and case-marked activity complements. The PDF text extraction has OCR
errors; it should not be copied as an answer source without checking the page.
This consultation supports the topic scope, not a claim that these app questions
were copied from or independently validated by the textbook.

In particular, do not turn this review into a rule that every subject must use
-mAk or that -mA always requires an overt person. The first item presents an
activity noun; choosing between all possible nominal readings is not its task.
Keep the user's requested focus on general activities for any follow-up usage
questions, rather than introducing possessed subordinate subjects here.
